Why Creators Need Community, Not Just Followers

5 min read  ·  Lore of Ambition

Having followers isn't the same thing as having a community.

A follower might watch your video. A community member might ask what you're building next. A follower might like your post. A community member might help you solve a problem. A follower can increase a number. A community can change the direction of a project.

The lonely side of creating

Building something can be surprisingly isolating. You can spend hours coding an application that nobody around you understands. You can spend weeks making art while your friends are doing something completely different. You can work on a business without knowing anyone else going through the same problems.

The internet technically gives us access to millions of people. But finding the right people can still be difficult.

Followers aren't necessarily collaborators

Most social platforms optimize for reach. That's useful when your primary goal is audience growth. But someone trying to build something may need something else. They might need:

  • feedback on what they're building
  • a collaborator or co-founder
  • a developer or designer
  • a mentor who's been through it before
  • an accountability partner
  • someone simply working on something similar

Those relationships don't necessarily come from chasing views. They come from finding people who share the same direction.

The idea behind the Bulletin

One of Lore's concepts is the Bulletin — a space where builders can put out what they actually need. Looking for a designer? Need feedback on a prototype? Want to find people participating in the same challenge? Instead of hoping the right person happens to see your post, the goal is to make those intentions more visible.

Community changes the experience

Building alone can feel like pushing a rock uphill. Building alongside other people doesn't necessarily make the work easy. But it makes the journey less lonely. You get to see other people struggling with their own projects. You get ideas. You exchange feedback. You celebrate milestones. And sometimes you meet someone who becomes part of the project itself.

That's the kind of community Lore of Ambition wants to explore. Not an audience watching builders from a distance. Builders watching, helping and growing alongside other builders.
