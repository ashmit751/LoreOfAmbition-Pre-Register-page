Why Social Media Rewards Results, Not the Journey

5 min read  ·  Lore of Ambition

Most social platforms are built around moments.

The finished project. The successful launch. The viral video. The perfect photograph. The achievement.

And there's nothing wrong with celebrating those things.

The problem is that the finished result hides almost everything that happened before it.

A developer might spend three months building an application, but the post announcing its launch might get more attention than the hundreds of hours that went into creating it. An artist might spend weeks learning a new technique, but only the finished painting gets posted. A student might spend months learning something difficult, but the internet usually sees the certificate rather than the struggle that came before it.

The process disappears.

That's one of the ideas behind Lore of Ambition. Lore is being built around a simple question:

What did you build today?

Instead of treating progress as something that only matters once it becomes impressive, the idea is to make progress itself worth documenting.

Your unfinished work matters

A first prototype might look terrible. A first Blender model might have terrible topology. Your first video might get ten views. Your first attempt at something can be objectively bad.

But those attempts are still evidence that you tried.

And eventually, those small pieces become a record of how you improved. That's what makes documenting a journey interesting. You don't just get to see where someone ended up — you get to see where they started.

Building in public is more than posting

Building in public has become increasingly popular among developers, founders and creators. But there's a difference between simply announcing what you're doing and actually documenting the journey.

"Working on my startup." "Day 14 of learning Blender." "Made my first prototype."

These posts can seem insignificant individually. Together, they create something much more valuable: a timeline.

Your timeline becomes evidence of consistency. It shows experiments, failures, improvements, decisions and milestones.

Social media doesn't have to be a highlight reel

The internet doesn't need another place where everyone pretends everything is going perfectly.

People are building businesses in bedrooms. Learning to code after college. Making games after work. Learning design from scratch. Starting YouTube channels with five subscribers. Training for something they've never done before.

Those stories are interesting precisely because they aren't finished.

Lore of Ambition is being designed around those people. Not people who already have an impressive story. People who are currently creating one.
